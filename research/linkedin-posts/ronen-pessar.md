## Post 1: Cold Call Openers That Actually Work

**Source:** https://www.mixmax.com/blog/interview-ronen-pessar  
**Also:** LinkedIn posts tagged #coldcalling #sdr

### Two Types of Openers Ronen Uses

**Option A: The Humorous Pattern Interrupt — "Roll the Dice"**
> "Hey, it's Ronen. And this is a cold call. Do you want to roll the dice?"

This disarms immediately by acknowledging the obvious (it's a cold call) and adding levity. It's polarizing — some people hang up, others laugh and stay on.

**Option B: Permission-Based Opener (More Reliable)**
> "Hey [Name], it's Ronen. Nobody likes getting a cold call, especially not on a Wednesday afternoon. May I share why I'm calling, and then you can let me know if we should continue talking? Is that fair?"

This works because:
- Acknowledges the interruption (respect)
- Gives the prospect control (agency)
- Gets explicit micro-commitment ("Is that fair?") before pitching

### When to Use Each
- Use **Pattern Interrupt** when calling a technical/builder audience that tends to be curious
- Use **Permission-Based** as the default — it's repeatable and converts better in most contexts

## Post 2: The Two-Phase Outbound System

**Source:** https://contactout.com/Ronen-Pessar-82259534; Mixmax interview; Outbound Operators content

### Phase 1: Live Cold Call Pilot
Before hiring an SDR team, Ronen runs a **3-hour live pilot** himself (or with the founder):
- Goal: 20–40% connect rate
- Tests the script, ICP, and value prop in real conditions
- Produces data on what's working before investing in headcount

### Phase 2: SDR Team Deployment (90 Days)
Once the pilot validates the model:
- Recruit for specific traits (not just "sales experience")
- Train on the tested script and framework
- Measure and iterate within 90 days

### Why This Matters
Most outbound programs fail because they hire first, test second. The two-phase system reverses this — you know the playbook works before you build the team.

## Post 3: Traits of Top-Performing SDRs

**Source:** https://www.mixmax.com/blog/interview-ronen-pessar  
**Also:** Sellbetter.xyz speaker profile

### The Five Traits
1. **Growth Mindset** — Views challenges as learning opportunities. Ronen tests for this in interviews by asking: "Tell me about a difficult personal truth you've had to confront."
2. **Self-Awareness** — Knows their weaknesses and works on them with humility. Doesn't blame the list, the product, or the market.
3. **Aligned Motivation** — Drive connected to the team's mission, not just personal commission. Sustains performance through rejection cycles.
4. **Adaptability** — Thrives in changing environments, especially startups. References "Who Moved My Cheese?" as the mental model.
5. **Chutzpah** — Shameless audacity. Confidently challenges ideas to establish peer-level respect with prospects, while remaining professional.

### Interview Question That Reveals All Five
> "Walk me through a time you were wrong about something important and what you did about it."

A candidate who answers specifically, without defensiveness, and who demonstrates they updated their behavior — that's your hire.

## Post 4: Overcoming Cold Call Objections

**Source:** Mixmax interview

### The Two Types of Objections
1. **Brush-offs** — "Email me," "I'm busy," "Not interested" — resistance to being contacted, not genuine objections
2. **Real objections** — "We already use X" or "We don't have budget" — specific product/situation concerns

### Handling Brush-Offs
Use **pattern interruption**: change your tone unexpectedly.
- For interpersonal roles: shift to warm and friendly
- For technical roles: shift to sharp and direct

**Example response to "Can you just email me?":**
> "I've been doing this a while... still waiting to hear back from my emails. Could we find 30 minutes Monday afternoon or Tuesday morning to talk?"

This is honest, slightly self-deprecating, and reframes the email deflection as futile without being aggressive.

### Handling "We Already Have a Solution"
Shift from product to problem:
> "When it comes to [core problem], can you guarantee [specific outcome]?"

This opens dialogue on gaps in their current solution without attacking it directly.

## Post 5: Psychological Preparation for Cold Calling

**Source:** Mixmax interview; LinkedIn posts

### Physical Techniques That Actually Help
- **Stand while calling** — significantly increases energy and tonality
- **Power poses before dialing** — reduces cortisol, boosts confidence (Amy Cuddy's research)
- **Don't call in isolation** — use a call floor or virtual call group; social energy is contagious

### The Mindset Reframe
- A **10% success rate** (1 meeting per 10 dials) is solid, professional performance
- Rejection is a **lagging indicator of volume**, not a reflection of quality
- Focus on **controllable inputs** (dials made, scripts tested) not outcomes (meetings booked)

### The "Shameless Audacity" Framework
Ronen's concept of chutzpah applies to the outbound mindset:
> "A true outbound mindset is the belief that you can build trust with a total stranger."

This isn't arrogance — it's an operating belief. If you don't believe you can add value to someone who doesn't know you, every dial will feel like an imposition.
