## Post 1: 30 B2B Cold Calling Tips for Sales Success (2026)

**Source:** https://close.com/blog/b2b-cold-calling-tips  
**Published:** 2026

### Before the Call (13 Tips)

**1. Use Cell Phone Numbers**  
Target mobile numbers over desk phones. Prospects always have their phones with them.

**2. Create a Cold Calling Blueprint**  
Develop a script-like guide (not to be read robotically) that outlines your desired outcome — booking appointments, scheduling demos, or discovery calls. Include different conversation pathways for various objection scenarios.

**3. Conduct Pre-Call Research**  
Investigate LinkedIn, Google, and industry sources. Look for: pain points, job titles, recent company announcements, funding rounds, or regulatory changes that make your outreach timely.

**4. Identify the Right Decision-Maker**  
Focus on job titles with purchasing influence — typically VPs or Directors. Avoid administrative gatekeepers and low-level staff who can't say yes.

**5. Check CRM History**  
Review your database to avoid redundant calls and identify inactive opportunities from 6+ months ago that may now be ready to re-engage.

**6. Warm Leads with Email First**  
Send emails before calling to build awareness. Track opens to prioritize prospects showing interest. Build a 4–6 week coordinated email + calling cadence.

**7. Leverage LinkedIn Connections**  
Optimize your profile as a consultant, not a salesperson. Connect with prospects beforehand. Start conversations to potentially schedule calls directly on LinkedIn.

**8. Build a Daily Action Plan**  
Time-block: email outreach in one block, research in another, calling during your peak energy hours (when prospects answer most).

**9. Establish a Strategic Cadence**  
Pre-schedule coordinated email and call sequences in your CRM. Use automated workflows to launch predetermined outreach patterns when leads are enrolled.

**10. Prepare a Gatekeeper Script**  
Write separate dialogue for administrative assistants. Explain why connecting with leadership benefits the company, not just you. Turn gatekeepers into allies.

**11. Develop the Right Mindset**  
Overcome fear through meditation, exercise, or motivational content. Embrace failure as learning, not defeat.

**12. Adopt a "No Quota" Approach**  
Set targets for rejections rather than successes. Expecting a specific number of "nos" daily reduces the sting and increases overall "yes" outcomes through higher volume.

**13. Use a Sales Dialer**  
Automate call routing with predictive or power dialers. Connect only when someone answers. Dramatically increases call volume efficiency.

### During the Call (11 Tips)

**14. Eliminate All Distractions**  
Close all applications except your dialer and CRM.

**15. Master Your Tone of Voice**  
Tone conveys 38% of communication vs. 7% from words. Practice voice training or role-playing to sound confident.

**16. Nail Your Opening Line**  
State your name, reason for calling, how you found them, and how you can help — all within seconds.

**17. Ask Open-Ended Questions and Listen Actively**  
Drive conversations through thoughtful questions that help prospects recognize their own problems. Actually listen to the answers.

**18. Embrace Strategic Silence**  
Pause after asking questions. Don't fill silence with additional prompts.

**19. Delay Your Sales Pitch**  
Build rapport first. Uncover at least one key need. Then tailor your pitch to that need.

**20. Emphasize Pain Points Over Features**  
Focus on problems your solution relieves rather than product specifications.

**21. Establish Value Before Discussing Price**  
When asked about cost, deflect: "Before quoting, I need to understand your specific needs." Justify price after establishing value.

**22. Build Trust with Tight-Lipped Prospects**  
Address tension directly: "I bet you don't trust me because I'm a sales rep. But if you let me know about [pain point], I'm confident I could help."

**23. Perfect Your Voicemail Technique**  
Keep messages brief. State your phone number at the beginning. Answer: Who are you? Why calling? What value? How to reach you?

**24. Close by Scheduling the Next Conversation**  
End cold calls by booking a deeper discovery call or product demo. "Instead of email chains, let's schedule 30 minutes to fully explain our solution."

---

### After the Call (6 Tips)

**25. Analyze Call Success Systematically**  
Two metrics: Are next steps clearly defined? Is the prospect excited about those steps? Both "yes" = success.

**26. Execute Strategic Follow-Up**  
Send meeting invites immediately for scheduled calls. For non-booked calls, implement pre-planned follow-up cadences. Continue following up monthly until explicit rejection.

**27. Track and Document Objections**  
Spreadsheet: common objections + successful responses. Share with team to collectively improve.

**28. Recover Quickly from Difficult Interactions**  
Don't let harsh rejections derail your day. One bad call should not affect the next.

**29. Monitor Key Performance Indicators**  
Calls made, pickup rates, qualifying conversations, meetings scheduled. Track over time, not just in snapshots.

**30. Maintain Persistent Effort**  
Cold calling is a numbers game. More calls = higher success probability. Consistency beats perfection.

## Post 2: Steli's Core Philosophy on Cold Calling

**Source:** https://www.saastock.com/blog/how-to-design-a-killer-saas-sales-call-steli-efti/

### The "Never Stop Until YES or NO" Rule
Silence is **not** rejection. A prospect who hasn't responded hasn't said no — they're busy.

> "You should never stop until you get a YES or NO answer."

This means:
- Following up 8–12+ times is not aggressive, it's professional
- Stopping after 2 emails is giving up too early
- The right cadence continues across multiple channels until clarity

### The Minimum Viable Response Rate
- **Answering rate:** aim for minimum 15% of calls picked up
- **Leads-to-something rate:** among people who answer, 15% should result in a next step
- If you're below these benchmarks, something in your targeting or opening is broken

### Selling to Startup Founders
Steli's four core principles for B2B SaaS (from x27marketing.com):
1. **Create the right offer** — speak to what they actually care about, not what you sell
2. **Find your early adopters first** — don't try to sell to everyone; find the 20% who are naturally curious
3. **Be obsessively helpful** — give value before, during, and after the sale
4. **Follow up relentlessly** — most people give up way too early; persistence is a competitive advantage
