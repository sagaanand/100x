## Post 1: What 1 Million Cold Emails Taught About B2B SaaS Sales

**Source:** https://saasclub.io/podcast/alex-berman-inspirebeats/  
**Also:** https://blitzmetrics.com/alex-berman-the-cold-email-king/

### Core Insight: Personalization Beats Volume, Every Time
Alex sent over 1 million cold emails. His key finding:

> Personalized cold emails convert at **7–8%**. Template emails convert at **1%**.

The math: if you send 100 personalized emails instead of 1,000 templates, you get the same number of responses in 1/10th the time — and those responses are from better-fit prospects.

### The 2-Minute Personalization Method
Spend exactly 2 minutes per email:
- **Minute 1:** Review their company website. Write a custom first line referencing something specific about their product, team, or recent announcement.
- **Minute 2:** Check their LinkedIn or Twitter. Find a personal PS — a side project, a shared connection, an interest.
- **The middle:** Keep the pitch the same across all emails (your offer doesn't change).

### Why This Works
The custom first line signals: "I know who you are, I didn't just buy a list." The personal PS signals: "I'm a real person who found something specific about you." The middle can be templated because by that point, they're reading.

## Post 2: The 3C Framework

**Source:** https://alexberman.com; Referenced in multiple interviews

### The Three C's
1. **Compliment** — Something genuine and specific about their work
2. **Case Study** — What you've done for a similar company (specific numbers preferred)
3. **CTA** — One low-friction question (not "book a call")

### Email Example Using 3C
**Subject:** Your recent funding round

> [Name], loved how you framed the problem in your TechCrunch interview about [specific topic] — that's a perspective I haven't seen many founders articulate clearly.
>
> We helped [Similar Company] scale their outbound from 5 to 47 qualified demos per month in 90 days using a signal-based prospecting system.
>
> Would it be useful to see how we built that for them?

**Why each element matters:**
- Compliment shows research (not flattery)
- Case study shows proof (not claims)
- CTA asks for curiosity, not commitment

## Post 3: Evaluating B2B Market Segments with Cold Email

**Source:** https://leanb2bbook.com/blog/alex-berman-b2b-market-segments/  
**Also:** https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/20708601

### The Cold Email as Market Research Tool
Before committing to an ICP, Alex recommends using cold email to test 3–5 market segments simultaneously:

1. Write a different value proposition for each segment
2. Send 50–100 emails per segment
3. Measure: open rate, reply rate, and meeting conversion

**What the data tells you:**
- High open rate, low reply = subject line works, but offer doesn't resonate
- Low open rate = wrong list or weak subject line
- High reply rate = you've found a real pain point

### The ICP Validation Protocol
- **Minimum viable test:** 50 emails per segment
- **Decision threshold:** If one segment gets 3x the reply rate of others, double down on it
- **Timeline:** 2–3 weeks to have signal on 5 segments

This process costs almost nothing and generates real market data — faster and cheaper than customer interviews.

## Post 4: Agency Cold Email vs. SaaS Cold Email

**Source:** Referenced across multiple AlexBerman.com content pieces

### Key Differences

| Agency Outreach | SaaS Outreach |
|---|---|
| Higher-touch, more personal | Can scale with semi-automation |
| Focus on specific deliverable | Focus on category of problem |
| CTA = strategy call | CTA = demo or trial |
| Target: founder/VP | Target: specific job title |

### What Transfers Between Both
1. **Specificity** — generic doesn't work in either category
2. **Social proof** — case studies matter more than claims
3. **Low-friction CTA** — never ask for a 45-minute call in a cold email
4. **Volume discipline** — 50 quality emails beats 500 blasted templates

## Post 5: How to Follow Up Without Being Annoying

**Source:** Alex Berman YouTube channel; referenced content

### The Follow-Up System
Most cold email programs fail not on the first email but on follow-up. Alex's approach:

**Follow-up 1 (Day 3):** Add one new piece of value
> "Thought this [article/case study] might be relevant given what I mentioned."

**Follow-up 2 (Day 7):** Direct and simple
> "Wanted to bump this up in case it got buried."

**Follow-up 3 (Day 14):** Perspective shift
> "I'm going to shift gears — would it make more sense to connect in Q3 when you're planning for [next year/initiative]?"

**Follow-up 4 (Day 21):** Break-up email
> "I'll assume now isn't the right time. I'll check back in 6 months — but if anything changes before then, here's my direct line."

### Key Principle
Each follow-up should add something — a new insight, a different angle, or a timing shift. Not just "checking in."
