## Post 1: The #1 Outbound Mistake

**Source:** https://www.mixmax.com/blog/interview-founder-ceo-jason-bay-outbound-squad

### The Problem
Most SDRs send generic, pitchy cold emails because they misunderstand the purpose of outbound.

> "The real goal isn't finding people to sell to — it's finding people whose priorities align with the priorities that you can help with."

### Implication
Outbound shouldn't start with your product. It starts with identifying where your capabilities address existing business priorities. That alignment is what generates meaningful conversations, not clever subject lines.

## Post 2: Self-Accountability Culture in Sales Teams

**Source:** Same interview

### Jason's Framework
Top performers don't need more manager coaching — they need a culture of self-coaching.

His approach: **"In order to get coaching from you, a rep must attempt to coach themselves."**

This means:
- Reps complete scorecards on their own calls before requesting feedback
- Managers coach on patterns, not individual plays
- Self-assessment reduces dependency and builds professional maturity

### Why It Matters for Outbound
Reps who self-coach catch their own pitch-first language, lack of research, and weak CTAs faster than managers can. This produces better outbound sequences at scale.

## Post 3: The Cold Call Framework

**Source:** Same interview

### Jason's 5-Step Cold Call Structure
1. **Research on LinkedIn and company news** — find a hook before dialing
2. **Open with a relevant observation + permission request** — don't just launch into pitch
3. **Deliver a 30-second hypothesis-based pitch** — "I think you might be experiencing X..."
4. **Ask diagnostic questions** — uncover actual pain, don't assume it
5. **Define specific next steps with easy scheduling** — don't leave it vague

### Key Metrics to Track
- **Answer Success Rate (ASR):** Percentage of dials answered by a human
- **Sales Conversion Rate:** Conversations that result in the desired next action
- **Cost Per Call:** Total operating cost ÷ calls made

## Post 4: The "Too Good To Ignore" Framework

**Source:** https://outboundsquad.com/too-good-to-ignore/

### Background
Built after analyzing 250+ sales teams. The finding: top performers generate **4.3x more pipeline** than average reps — not by making more calls, but by executing outbound at an exceptionally high standard.

### The Three Pillars

**1. Disqualify**  
Ruthlessly eliminate opportunities that don't warrant investment of time, energy, or attention. Most SDRs try to qualify everyone — top performers filter fast.

**2. Offer**  
Demonstrate consultative selling by providing strategic value early through meaningful "gives" to buyers — before asking for anything. Be a resource, not a vendor.

**3. Provoke**  
Challenge buyers with a compelling perspective on their challenges that drives immediate action. Make them feel the cost of inaction.

## Post 6: Strength as Manager's Blind Spot

**Source:** Same Mixmax interview

### The Paradox
Your greatest strengths as an individual contributor become **blind spots as a manager**.

If you're naturally great at building rapport, you'll assume others can do it too — so you won't teach it. If you're naturally curious, you'll forget that curiosity is a skill, not a trait.

### Implication for Cold Outreach Training
When building an outbound team:
1. Map what your top rep does that's "obvious" to them
2. Treat those obvious things as skills to be taught explicitly
3. Never assume something is "just personality" — it can always be systematized

- **The Challenger Sale** — "commercial teaching" as the core differentiation in outbound
