## Post 1: The One Thing That Kills Cold Email Response Rates

**Source:** https://joshbraun.com/one-thing-that-kills-cold-email-response-rates/

### Core Insight
**Formal writing kills response rates.** The problem isn't personalization, subject lines, or send times — it's that most cold emails sound like this:

> "We help RevOps reclaim the power of incentives with a platform built for revenue-focused teams."

Formal language triggers the reader's brain to recognize it as a sales pitch, not a conversation — so they don't respond.

### The Fix: The Coffee Test
Write your cold email as if you're explaining your offer to a friend over coffee. When the writing sounds conversational, the reader's brain subconsciously says: *"I'm in a conversation — I need to hold up my end and respond."*

### What This Looks Like in Practice
A 6-email sequence for CaptivateIQ that used:
- Reference-based credibility ("I know you through...")
- Casual questions rather than pitches
- Light humor and self-deprecating follow-ups:
  - *"How have you gone so long without responding to me? I'm such a delight."*
  - *"Still waiting to hear back from emails. Is this Tuesday good?"*

### Bottom Line
Authenticity and casual tone significantly outperform formal business language. Personality is not unprofessional — generic is.

## Post 2: Let's Write a Good Cold Email (The 7-Step Framework)

**Source:** https://joshbraun.com/lets-write-a-good-cold-email-4/

### The Framework
1. **Identify the person** — Be specific (e.g., homeowner, VP Sales)
2. **Define their job** — What are they trying to accomplish?
3. **Current reality** — How are they solving it today?
4. **The pain** — What results from that current approach?
5. **List alternatives** — What else could they use?
6. **Why alternatives fail** — What's wrong with each?
7. **Present your solution** — How does your product address the gap?

### The Email Example (Cordless MiniSaw)
**Subject:** 5522 Marbella Drive

> Honestly now did you spend your youth dreaming about someday spending your Sunday afternoon trimming trees on Marbella Drive? Ten million homeowners use the Cordless Mini Saw to trim five trees in 30 minutes. 6.5 hour charge. No $100 per tree landscaper. Worth a peek?

**Why it works:** Specificity (street address), social proof (10M homeowners), concrete value prop, zero jargon, low-friction CTA.

## Post 3: Be Cheeky

**Source:** https://joshbraun.com/be-cheeky/

### Core Principle
Humor is not inappropriate in B2B cold outreach. Executives who read your email are the same people who watch comedy shows and scroll memes. Their sense of humor doesn't disappear at work.

> "When you make a prospect smile, their brain subconsciously says, 'Hey, that made me feel good; I'm going to respond.'"

### What Works
- **Cheeky humor:** Lighthearted teasing
- **Self-deprecating humor:** Poking fun at yourself

### Real Examples
Follow-up lines that work:
- *"John, you don't have to wait three days to reply."*
- *"How have you gone so long without responding to me? I'm a delight."*

Sign-offs from Jon Buchan (cheeky master):
- *"Awaiting your fair but stern reply."*
- *"Awaiting your profanity-filled response."*

### Takeaway
Send emails with personality. Generic, emotionally devoid emails are forgettable. Cheeky emails get remembered, shared, and replied to.

## Post 4: Killer Cold Email That Landed

**Source:** https://joshbraun.com/killer-cold-email-that-landed/

### The Email That Got Josh's Attention
A pitch for content repurposing services that:
1. Opened with specific praise about Josh's sales anxiety video
2. Framed an opportunity as "untapped" (TikTok) — not a gap or failure
3. Referenced a specific case study (@abhormozi: 0 to 23K followers in 2 months)
4. Showed a demo mockup instead of claiming expertise
5. Closed with: *"Worth exploring?"* — not "Can we hop on a 30-minute call?"

### Why Each Element Worked
| Element | Why it worked |
|---|---|
| Specific compliment | Showed they actually watched the content |
| "Have you considered" framing | Ego-friendly — positions as opportunity not mistake |
| Named case study | Social proof via bandwagon effect |
| Demo preview | Showing > telling; addresses expertise objection pre-emptively |
| "Without lifting a finger" | Curiosity hook — unexplained = click |
| Low-friction ask | Gauging interest beats demanding calendar time |

### Universal Formula
1. Specific observation (reference real content they made)
2. Ego-friendly opportunity frame ("have you considered" not "you're missing")
3. Relevant social proof
4. Proof of concept (show, don't claim)
5. Curiosity gap
6. Low-friction CTA

## Post 5: 4T Framework for Cold Email (LinkedIn Post)

**Source:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-braun_heres-a-cold-email-framework-that-will-help-activity-6487124033203691520---1M  
**Also covered in:** https://www.luru.app/post/4-t-framework-cold-emails

### The 4 T's
| T | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | What caused you to reach out now? | "Saw your funding announcement" |
| **Think** | What insight do you bring? | "Most Series A companies struggle with..." |
| **Third-Party** | Credibility reference or case study | "We worked with [similar company] and..." |
| **Talk** | Low-friction invitation to conversation | "Would it make sense to explore this?" |

### Why It Works
Most cold emails lead with the seller's needs. The 4T Framework leads with the buyer's situation. Trigger shows you did research. Think shows you add value. Third-Party reduces risk. Talk lowers the barrier to respond.

### Key Principle
Shift focus from "what you sell" to "what they care about." The email should feel like a thoughtful conversation opener, not a pitch.
