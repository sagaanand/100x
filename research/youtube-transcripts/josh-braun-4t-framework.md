## Video Topic: Overcoming Prospect Resistance with the 4T Framework

### Why Prospect Resistance Exists
When you cold email a stranger, resistance is the default. The prospect is skeptical because:
- They don't know you
- They've received hundreds of generic pitches before
- They assume you're going to pitch them immediately

The 4T Framework is designed to disarm that resistance before it fully activates.

---

## The 4T Framework — Detailed Breakdown

### T1: Trigger
**What it is:** The reason you're reaching out, framed in a way that shows you did research.

**What makes a good Trigger:**
- Specific observation about their situation (not generic)
- References something they posted, announced, or did
- Shows you understand their world, not just their job title

**Examples:**
- "Noticed your team recently expanded into enterprise accounts..."
- "Saw your post about SDR productivity challenges..."
- "Your recent funding round suggests you're scaling the team..."

**What makes a bad Trigger:**
- "I noticed you're the VP of Sales at [Company]" — this is not a trigger, it's a statement of what you know from LinkedIn
- "I wanted to reach out because..." — about you, not them

---

### T2: Think
**What it is:** A question or observation that makes the prospect pause and reflect on their situation.

**Goal:** Evoke a "Hmm, I'm not sure" response — not a "Yes, obviously" or "No, not relevant."

**The technique:** Highlight a gap between what most companies do and what the best ones do.

**Examples:**
- "Most SDR teams I talk to spend 60% of their time on research, leaving 40% for actual outreach. Does that match what you're seeing?"
- "Have you found a way to maintain personalization quality as you scale from 100 to 1,000 emails per month?"
- "When it comes to reply rates, can your current approach consistently hit 10%?"

**Why questions work better than statements:** Questions invite the prospect into a conversation. Statements invite them to agree or disagree — and strangers default to disagree.

---

### T3: Third-Party Credibility
**What it is:** Social proof that makes your claim believable.

**Why it matters:** People are more likely to try something if people like them already tried it. The "bandwagon effect" is real.

**What works:**
- Specific company names (even better if it's a known name in their industry)
- Specific numbers (not "we increased revenue" but "23% more pipeline in 60 days")
- Relevant comparison (a company in a similar segment, size, or stage)

**Examples:**
- "Teams like [Company X] and [Company Y] use this approach and are seeing 15%+ reply rates on cold outreach."
- "[Company] went from 3 to 47 qualified demos per month in 90 days."

**What doesn't work:**
- Vague social proof: "We work with companies like yours"
- Generic numbers: "Increased efficiency by 30%" (30% of what?)

---

### T4: Talk
**What it is:** A low-pressure invitation to continue the conversation.

**Key principle:** Give people the freedom to say no. Paradoxically, this increases the yes rate.

**The contrast:**
- High-pressure CTA: "Let's schedule a 30-minute call next Tuesday to walk through our platform"
- Low-pressure CTA: "Would it be worth a quick conversation to see if this is relevant?"

**Josh's framing:**
> "You're gauging interest, not requesting a meeting. When you give people the freedom to choose, you lower resistance."

**Examples:**
- "Worth exploring?"
- "Relevant to what you're working on?"
- "Would it make sense to talk?"

## Full Email Example Using 4T

**Subject:** SDR research time

> James, noticed your SDRs target HR/Benefit Directors in companies with 500+ employees. [Trigger]
>
> Most teams I talk to spend 60% of their SDR hours on lead research rather than outreach. Does that match what you're seeing? [Think]
>
> Teams like [Company A] and [Company B] reclaimed 8 hours per SDR per week by automating the research phase, which freed them to double outreach volume. [Third-Party Credibility]
>
> Worth a quick call to see if that's relevant? [Talk]

---

## Why This Email Works
1. **First sentence is about them**, not about you
2. **Question creates engagement** without demanding commitment
3. **Specific numbers** make the proof believable
4. **CTA is frictionless** — "worth a quick call" is a much smaller ask than "schedule a 30-minute demo"
