## Core Cold Calling Philosophy

The primary mistake most reps make: **excessive self-focus**.

Most cold calls are a monologue about the company, the product, or the pitch. The prospect is treated as a target rather than a peer.

Jason's reframe: **reverse-engineer every call from the prospect's perspective**.

What does the prospect experience in the first 10 seconds?
- A stranger calling with no context
- Immediate assumption: this is a sales call, I need to get off
- Default response: "Send me an email"

The job of the cold call is to break that pattern — not with cleverness, but with genuine relevance.

## The Three-Step Cold Call Structure

### Step 1: Permission-Based Opening
> "Hey Sarah, it's Jason with XYZ company. I know I probably caught you in the middle of something. Do you have a minute?"

**Why this works:**
- Acknowledges the interruption rather than ignoring it
- Asks for micro-commitment ("do you have a minute?") before delivering any pitch
- Establishes a two-way conversation, not a monologue
- Lower resistance than launching directly into a pitch

**What not to do:**
- "How are you doing today?" (hollow, transactional)
- "I wanted to tell you about our product..." (seller-first, not prospect-first)
- "Is this a bad time?" (invites "yes, it is")

---

### Step 2: Relevant Research Reference
Frame your research as a question, not a statement.

**Instead of:** "I see you're hiring 10 SDRs right now."  
**Use:** "I noticed one of your big initiatives right now is scaling the SDR team. Is that right?"

The question version:
- Gives the prospect a chance to correct, confirm, or add context
- Signals that you're interested in their reality, not just your pitch
- Creates a collaborative dynamic rather than a confrontation

### Research Hierarchy (What to Research, In Priority Order)
1. **Individual-specific:** What has this person said publicly? What have they done recently?
2. **Persona-level:** What do people in their role commonly struggle with?
3. **Industry-level:** What's happening in their market that affects their priorities?
4. **Company-level:** Funding, headcount changes, product launches, news

Most reps research in reverse order (company first). The most effective calls reference individual-specific information first.

**Three categories to research:**
- Education (what they've shared about their approach or beliefs)
- Accomplishments (what they've recently done or built)
- Investments (what they're currently focused on)

---

### Step 3: The Priority Drop
After establishing context with research, drop into the problem:

> "The reason I'm calling is that I talk to a lot of people in [their situation], and I consistently hear [common problem 1] and [common problem 2]. Are either of those top of mind for you?"

**Why "priority drop" is the right frame:**
- You're not pitching a solution — you're testing if the problem exists
- If they say yes → natural entry into a discovery conversation
- If they say no → you've learned something useful, and you can ask what IS a priority

**Using customer language:**
Don't say "our platform enables revenue teams to optimize pipeline velocity." Say "I talk to a lot of VPs who feel like their SDRs are doing a ton of activity but not moving the right deals forward." Use their words, not your product description.

## The "Too Good To Ignore" Outbound Pitch Framework

**Source:** https://outboundsquad.com/too-good-to-ignore/

### The Three-Word Philosophy
Every element of your pitch should be so relevant, specific, and valuable that the prospect thinks: *"This is too good to ignore."*

### What This Looks Like in a Pitch

**Weak pitch:**
> "We help B2B SaaS companies improve their outbound pipeline with our AI-powered prospecting tool."

**"Too Good To Ignore" pitch:**
> "I work with VP Sales at Series B SaaS companies who are scaling from 5 to 20 SDRs. The consistent challenge I hear is that as the team grows, the quality of outreach drops — everyone's using the same templates and nobody's personalizing. Does that match what you're seeing?"

**The difference:**
- Specific company stage (Series B, 5 to 20 SDRs)
- Named the role (VP Sales)
- Described their exact problem in their language
- Asked a diagnostic question rather than pitching a solution

## Objection Handling Formula

**Empathize → Validate → Offer**

| Step | What to Do | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Empathize** | Acknowledge the concern as legitimate | "Completely understand — you hear from a lot of people." |
| **Validate** | Restate the objection with industry context | "Honestly, most of the VPs I talk to say the same thing. Outbound has gotten noisier." |
| **Offer** | Share a relevant customer story | "That's actually why [Company X] called us — and what they found was..." |

## Four-Step Sales Coaching Process (for SDR Leaders)

1. **Teach** — Use 5-minute micro-trainings rather than 60-minute sessions. Attention degrades. Specific > general.
2. **Practice** — Structured simulations where reps discover persona details through questions (not told upfront)
3. **Execute** — Group calling hours: reps call live while others watch and support
4. **Feedback** — Consistent cadence (daily or weekly). Reps self-assess first, then get manager input.

### The Self-Assessment Requirement
Before any rep brings a call to their manager for coaching, they must:
1. Listen to the recording themselves
2. Complete a self-assessment scorecard
3. Identify 2 things they did well and 1 thing they'd change

This creates self-aware reps who improve between sessions, not just during them.
